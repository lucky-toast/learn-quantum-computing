Side note after Lesson 5

S is a quarter turn

Multiplying by i rotates a number 90° in the complex plane. The S gate does exactly that to one amplitude.

Each dial is a complex plane. S leaves the top amplitude alone and multiplies the bottom one by i, so the bottom arrow turns while keeping its length. Four quarter turns make a full circle, so S⁴ = I.

Why multiplying by i rotates

Definition A complex number x + yi can be drawn as the point (x, y). The horizontal axis is the real part and the vertical axis is the imaginary part. The number i is defined by i² = −1.

Multiply any number by i and use that definition:

i(x + yi) = xi + yi² = −y + xi

So the point (x, y) moves to (−y, x). Drag z below and watch the dashed triangle. It keeps its shape and turns 90° counterclockwise.

The matrix under the plane is the same move in ordinary linear algebra. It is the standard 90° rotation matrix. Apply it twice and you get −I, a half turn, which is the geometric meaning of i² = −1.[1]

Every phase is a rotation

Math fact When complex numbers multiply, their lengths multiply and their angles add.[1] A number of length 1 therefore changes only the angle. It is a pure rotation.

Euler's formula names the number of length 1 at angle θ, measured in radians:[2]

eiθ = cos θ + i sin θ

At θ = π/2, a quarter turn, this gives 0 + 1·i = i. At θ = π, a half turn, it gives −1. So i and −1 are two stops on the same circle.

Applying gates adds their turns, so S · S = Z and T · T = S. To undo a turn, finish the circle: three more quarter turns land in the same place as one quarter turn backward, so S³ = S⁻¹, the gate with −i in the corner.

What the turn does physically

Postulate Measuring gives each outcome with probability equal to the amplitude's absolute value squared.[3] A rotation keeps the length, so a phase gate cannot change the odds of a direct measurement. That is why the first bar in the animation stays at 50%.

The angle shows up when amplitudes combine. Apply H to (1/√2, eiθ/√2). The top entry becomes (1 + eiθ)/2, an addition of two arrows:

P(0) = |1 + eiθ|² / 4 = (1 + cos θ) / 2

Arrows pointing the same way reinforce, giving 100%. Opposite arrows cancel, giving 0%. A quarter turn lands halfway, at 50%. This is your Lesson 1 design move in its general form: store information in an angle, then interfere to make it measurable.
