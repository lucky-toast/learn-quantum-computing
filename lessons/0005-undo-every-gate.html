Lesson 5

Undo every gate

Find a circuit by walking from the answer all the way back to the start.

Your goal. You are given a target state. Undo one gate at a time until you reach |00⟩, then read off the circuit that builds the target.

Why undoing is always possible

Postulate Quantum operations on a state are described by unitary matrices.[1]

Math fact Every unitary matrix U has an inverse, written U⁻¹, with U⁻¹U = I. Here I is the identity matrix, which leaves every state unchanged.[1] So no gate in a circuit destroys the information needed to step back.

You already know CNOT is its own inverse. Check H yourself. Multiply H by H and enter the four entries of the result.

Undo in reverse order

Suppose a circuit applies G₁, then G₂, then G₃. Each new gate multiplies on the left, so the target is:

|target⟩ = G₃ G₂ G₁ |00⟩

G₃ sits next to the target, so it is the only gate you can remove first. Multiply both sides by G₃⁻¹, and G₃⁻¹G₃ = I disappears:

G₃⁻¹ |target⟩ = G₂ G₁ |00⟩

Repeat for G₂ and G₁. X, Z, H, and CNOT are each their own inverse, so here undoing a gate means applying the same gate again.

Unwind three states

Given: a target state. Asked: choose a gate to undo, work out the state it leaves, and continue until you reach |00⟩. Use paper for each step. There can be more than one correct path.

Check the idea

This is your new design loop: undo a gate, simplify the state, repeat. A good next gate usually creates zeros or reveals a pair you recognize. When you reach |00⟩, the reversed list is your circuit.
