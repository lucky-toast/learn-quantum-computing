Lesson 1

See the invisible sign

Your first design problem, solved with a circuit you find yourself.

Your goal. A machine hands you one qubit. It is secretly in one of two states, and you must say which, with certainty, using a single measurement.

A qubit is a unit vector

A qubit's state is a column of two numbers called amplitudes. The sum of their squared magnitudes must equal 1.[1] Amplitudes can be negative, which is where this lesson's puzzle comes from.

|0⟩ = 10   |1⟩ = 01   |+⟩ = 1/√21/√2   |−⟩ = 1/√2−1/√2

Measuring gives 0 or 1. The chance of each is its amplitude's magnitude, squared.[1] So |+⟩ and |−⟩ both give a 50/50 coin flip. Measuring directly cannot tell them apart, and those are the two states in your puzzle.

Check the rule

Gates are matrices

A gate changes the state by matrix multiplication: multiply each row by the column, and add. Here is H acting on |0⟩:

H|0⟩ = 1/√2 111−1 10 = 1/√2 1·1 + 1·01·1 − 1·0 = 1/√21/√2 = |+⟩

You have three gates to work with:

X0110
Z100−1
H1/√2 111−1

Design it

Before you click, predict on paper what each gate does to |+⟩ and |−⟩. Then build a circuit that gives each input its own certain outcome. Gates apply left to right.

Now prove it

A circuit that works is good. Knowing why it works is the skill you came for. Compute H|−⟩ by hand:

H|−⟩ = 1/√2 111−1 1/√2−1/√2 = topbottom

Name the move

This is the core design move you will reuse: put the answer into a sign, then interfere so it becomes a measurable outcome.
